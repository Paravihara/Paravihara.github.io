*August 4th, 2023*

This article in the "Heading West Reaching East" series continues from "The Magic of Vision" and "On Constructing a Reality," further explaining how our six sense faculties present such a vibrant yet troubled world of body and mind. The reason for saying "present" rather than "perceive" is that after a deep analysis of the visual system, the materialist illusion that "the eye is like a camera, allowing us to see an objective world outside" has been dismantled.

At the end of "The Magic of Vision," we asked: If biological senses are truly like a projector, casting a virtual reality for us similar to the Matrix in the so named movie (in other words, if the world of body and mind is truly our imagination), then why is there a "you" in my imagination and a "me" in yours? Moreover, why is the world I project almost identical to the world others project?

![Illustration from the original manuscript](/images/heading-west-reaching-east/image54.png)

Heinz von Foerster, the founder of second-order cybernetics, did not provide a convincing answer to this thorny question in "On Constructing a Reality." To answer this, philosophical speculation by scientists is insufficient; it requires the wisdom of the East, derived from actual experiential practice. In other words, although scientists can understand through analyzing the sensory system that the body, the mind and the world is a projection of a circular feedback loop, they lack the actual experience of meditative practice. They cannot, like practitioners, gain the ability to truly alter the content of this projection, and thus cannot experience all possible steady states of this "feedback loop." The above questions can only be resolved after fully comprehending all possible steady states.

![Illustration from the original manuscript](/images/heading-west-reaching-east/image16.png)

In the Pali Canon suttas, these different steady states of projection are summarized as the Seven Abodes of Consciousness:

1. The Abode of Consciousness with Multiple Bodies and Multiple Perceptions: A steady projection corresponding to the Sensual Realm, including the sensual-realm devas and our world filled with humans and animals.

2. The Abode of Consciousness with Multiple Bodies and Single Perception: A steady projection corresponding to the Brahma Heaven, i.e., the state of the First Jhānas (meditative absorption) in the Form Realm.

3. The Abode of Consciousness with Single Body and Multiple Perceptions: A steady corresponding to the Abhassara (Radiant) Deva, i.e., the states from the Second to the Third Jhānas in the Form Realm.

4. The Abode of Consciousness with Single Body and Single Perception: A steady state corresponding to the Subhakṛtsna (All-Pure) Deva, i.e., the state of the Fourth Jhānas in the Form Realm.

5. The Abode of Consciousness of the Sphere of Infinite Space: A steady state without a body, possessing only the concept of "infinite space," corresponding to the Sphere of Infinite Space in the Formless Realm.

6. The Abode of Consciousness of the Sphere of Infinite Consciousness: A steady state without a body, possessing only the concept of "infinite consciousness/awareness/mind," corresponding to the Sphere of Infinite Consciousness in the Formless Realm.

7. The Abode of Consciousness of the Sphere of Nothingness: A steady state of the concept of "there is nothing," corresponding to the Sphere of Nothingness in the Formless Realm.

These Seven Abodes of Consciousness, encompassing the Sensual Realm, Form Realm, and Formless Realm, precisely correspond to the ancient Indian world model depicted below.

![Illustration from the original manuscript](/images/heading-west-reaching-east/image55.png)

In the illustration, the giant serpent biting its own tail, representing the noumenon of the world, is named Asootee. This coincides remarkably with the "circular feedback loop" capable of generating all phenomena in second-order cybernetics. The various levels of worlds in the illustration, from bottom to top, are precisely the different Stations of Consciousness (projection steady states) from the hells up to the Brahma Heavens, spanning the Sensual, Form, and Formless Realms.

- The Sensual Realm, as the name suggests, is a world of body and mind (a steady state) generated by the drive of sensual desire. In terms of sensory feeling, it is a world of mixed pleasure and pain. The heavier the desires and the more the clinging, the greater the suffering. The natural disasters, national conflicts, epidemic diseases triggered by the great development of modern material civilization, as well as the increasingly widespread depression, are concrete manifestations of this counter-intuitive casual law.

- The Form Realm is a world of body and mind (a steady state) driven by thoughts free from desire. In terms of sensory feeling, it is a world of pure pleasure without suffering. The First Jhānas's "joy and pleasure born of seclusion (from desire)," the Second Jhānas's "joy and pleasure born of concentration," the Third Jhānas's "blissful ease free from joy," and the Fourth Jhānas's "purification of equanimity and mindfulness" are all projection steady states that can be entered through diligent practice of abandoning greed, concentrating and relaxing the body and mind.

- The Formless Realm is a metaphysical world where "all phenomena return to the One," formed by transcendent, non-dualistic concepts such as "emptiness," "consciousness/awareness/mind," and "nothingness." This is not the "emptiness and nothingness" understood in opposition to "existence," but a transcendent, absolute, "originally not a single thing exists" kind of "emptiness and nothingness." Concrete examples of the Formless Realm can be found in the exemplary demeanor of the patriarchs in Zen koans.

In the Buddhist system, these Three Realms and the Seven Abodes of Consciousness are collectively called the "world of suffering" or the "near shore of samsara (cycle of rebirth)," because they are all impermanent phenomena arising from different delusions. Clinging to the impermanent ultimately results in suffering. Yet the Buddha tells us that the "freedom from suffering and attainment of happiness" that everyone wants but finds elusive is actually not far away. When one fully comprehends that these Seven Stations of the worldly existence are all "dependently arisen, impermanent, suffering, empty, non-self, non-other, and does not have containment relationship with me," the mind, through non-clinging, will tacitly accord with the "far shore" of ultimate liberation.
