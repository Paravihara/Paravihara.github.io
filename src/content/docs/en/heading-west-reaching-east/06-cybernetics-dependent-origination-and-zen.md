*August 8th, 2023*

![Illustration from the original manuscript](/images/heading-west-reaching-east/image16.png)

In the previous article, "Cybernetics, the Seven Abodes of Consciousness, and the Ancient Indian World Model," we briefly outlined the intrinsic connections between the "doughnut" bio-cognitive model of second-order cybernetics, the Buddhist Dhamma's Seven Abodes of Consciousness, and the ancient Indian world model. In the Buddhist Dhamma, "consciousness" (識) encompasses both mental and material aspects, yet is distinct from both. In the language of Dependent Origination (緣起法), it is said: "Conditioned by name-and-form (名色), consciousness arises; conditioned by consciousness, name-and-form arises." These three mutually condition each other's arising; none are the "primary" substance, controller, or true Self.

Among these three, "name" (名) belongs to the mental side, "form" (色) belongs to the material side, and "consciousness" (識) stands between them. The mutual conditioning and arising of these three "things" give rise to the various physical-mental worlds of the Sensual Realm, Form Realm, and Formless Realm. What Laozi refers to in the Tao Te Ching as "Three gives birth to the myriad things" also points to these three. In the system of the I Ching, these three are referred to as "the Taichi (Supreme Polarity) generates the Two Modes (兩儀)" or "One Yin and one Yang is called the Tao."

![Illustration from the original manuscript](/images/heading-west-reaching-east/image55.png)

Roughly divided, sentient beings in the Sensual Realm mostly cling to "form" (e.g., materialism). Those in the Form Realm mostly cling to "name" (e.g., idealism). Those in the Formless Realm mostly cling to the "consciousness" that unifies all phenomena (e.g., existentialism, Brahman/Atman, God, the Great Dao, Emptiness, Buddha-nature, etc.).

The later Buddhist school of Consciousness-Only (唯識宗) further developed this "consciousness" into the all-encompassing "storehouse consciousness" (Alaya Consciousness or阿賴耶識), the eighth consciousness, regarding it as the substance of all mental and material phenomena—hence "all phenomena are consciousness-only" (萬法唯識). If one can fully comprehend the dependent nature of all things (dependent origination), one can break through the "barrier of consciousness and lock of emotions," transforming this "storehouse consciousness" into "the womb of the Tathāgata / Buddha-nature" (如來藏性/佛性)—this is called "turning consciousness into wisdom."

All this sounds very theoretical and abstract. For ordinary people, "name" and "form" are relatively easier to understand because they are still things that are "visible and tangible." However, the "consciousness" as the "substance" of name-and-form is very difficult to truly experience. Without personal experience of "consciousness," merely knowing the nominal concepts makes it hard to truly enter the perspective of Eastern wisdom; all discussions become mere intellectual play.

Traditional methods involve years of seated meditation and Zen investigation to personally experience this "substance/nature" of consciousness that unifies all phenomena. Here, we introduce a simpler, more direct method: in the present moment, turn awareness back to observe that "observer/awareness" within all the manifestations of mind, body, internal and external, mental and material—that "subject/self."... Please spend more time repeatedly experiencing this...

![Illustration from the original manuscript](/images/heading-west-reaching-east/image56.png)

Some people have sharp visual faculties and can experience that (visual) consciousness which can see brightness and darkness (by opening and closing the eyes) but is itself neither brightness nor darkness. Some have sharp auditory faculties and can experience that (auditory) consciousness which can hear sound and silence (e.g., by striking a bell or chime and follow the sound to silence) but transcends sound and silence. If you don't experience it, don't be discouraged. This is also inherently possessed by everyone; it's just because it's too close that we fail to experience it. At this point, you can try the blind spot experiment from "The Magic of Vision." The (absence of) sensation within the blind spot is actually very close to the state of the visual "consciousness" that transcends yet does not separate from brightness and darkness...

You may find this "consciousness/awareness/observer," although invisible and intangible, is "always present" and "omnipresent." Because if the "observer/consciousness" were not present, no (observed) mental or material phenomenon (name-and-form) could possibly exist. Yet, although this observer is always present and omnipresent, it is also "formless, signless, ultimately empty and non-existent." Because anything that is not empty, not non-existent, and has form and signs can only be an observed "name-and-form" object; it cannot be the observing "consciousness."

If one can experience this "consciousness," one can experience what Zen Master Linji (臨濟) meant by saying, "On this lump of red flesh, there is a True Man of no rank who is always going in and out of the faces of each one of you." Yet Patriarch Linji also said, "What is the True Man of no rank shit stick?" The implied meaning is that although this "consciousness" can be used as a tool to cut off afflictions (shit stick), it must not be clung to as the "Tathāgata" Within Linji's Three Sentences, the "person inside" referred to in the line "Just look at the puppet show on the stage; the pulling and manipulating all depends on the person inside," from the sentence about "being unable to save oneself," also points to this "consciousness" spirit.

![Illustration from the original manuscript](/images/heading-west-reaching-east/image57.png)

This "consciousness" is also the "central position" (正位) in the Caodong School's Five Ranks of Lord and Minister—"an old woman who has lost her way encounters an ancient mirror." "Consciousness" is compared to an "ancient mirror" because not only are all phenomena the "content" of this consciousness, but even time and space are objects observed by this "consciousness." Therefore, experiencing this "consciousness" might give one the illusion of transcending time and space, transcending life and death, and realizing the substance of the universe. Understanding that this "consciousness" is still part of dependent origination, impermanent, and unsatisfactory (dukkha), and letting go of attachment to it, one can understand why the "central position" is still not arriving home. Beyond it, there are still the "arrival within integration" (兼中至) and "integration within arrival" (兼中到), which involve "breaking the ancient mirror." From this, one can also experience that the original Buddhism's "Dependent Origination" and the wordless "Chinese Chan School" actually "breathe through the same nostrils."

![Illustration from the original manuscript](/images/heading-west-reaching-east/image58.png)

Simultaneously, this "consciousness" is also the core difference between first-order cybernetics and second-order cybernetics mentioned by von Foerster in the interview "Ethics and Second-Order Cybernetics": First-order cybernetics is about the "circular causal laws" of the observed phenomena, while second-order cybernetics is about the "circular causal laws" of the observer.

![Illustration from the original manuscript](/images/heading-west-reaching-east/image59.png)

Interestingly, first-order cybernetics, which studies observed phenomena (name-and-form), actually reveals the dependent origination (self-organizing) process of the observer (consciousness/self) (see "AI and the Hand of God"). Meanwhile, second-order cybernetics, which studies the dependent origination laws of the observer, reveals the dependent origination (self-organizing) process of the world (name-and-form) (see "The Magical Vision" and "On Constructing a Reality"). This once again coincides with and echoes from afar the principle of Dependent Origination: "Conditioned by name-and-form, consciousness arises; conditioned by consciousness, name-and-form arises."

Writing here, the "Heading West Reaching East" series is also nearing its end. The original intention of this series was to start from the cutting edge of Western modern cognitive science to build a bridge leading to Eastern wisdom, especially the Buddhist path of liberation. Its purpose is not to establish some advanced scientific theory or popularize science, but rather to use in-depth scientific examination and analysis to loosen the modern mind's deep-seated attachments to "name," "form," or "consciousness" as found in idealism, materialism or existentialism, etc.

These deeply rooted subconscious delusions and attachments are not only the root of life's predicaments—birth, aging, sickness, death, separation from loved ones, association with the disliked, not getting what one seeks—but also the greatest obstacles to realizing the true reality of the dependent origination of the mind, the body and the world, and to entering the ultimate shore of liberation.
